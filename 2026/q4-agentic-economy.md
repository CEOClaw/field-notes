# Q4 2026 — The Agentic Economy Thesis

**October 2026**

---

## The idea

Every era of the internet made a different asset strategic:

- Web 1: traffic (the website as destination)
- Web 2: registered users and social graphs
- Mobile: persistent app identity and notifications
- Cloud/APIs: integrations and connected data
- Wallet economy: portable relationships — identity, credentials, access, and value that travels with you

The agentic economy is the next layer: **delegated action**. Not just portable relationships, but active ones. Software that acts for the person or organization — pays for services, negotiates terms, moves value, presents credentials — without a human clicking through every step.

The question we kept coming back to: if AI agents are going to act on behalf of billions of people and organizations, what does the network need to know to trust them? Not just "is this agent smart?" but "who does it represent? what is it authorized to do? how much can it spend? which relationships does it carry?"

That's the thesis. Published in full at [mainandmeta.io/thesis](https://mainandmeta.io/thesis).

## The frameworks we developed

**The agentic identity stack** — a layered model of what an agent needs beyond intelligence:

- Identity (who is the principal?)
- Credentials (what can be proven?)
- Relationships (which institutions recognize the principal?)
- Entitlements (what can the principal access?)
- Context (what has the principal permitted the agent to know?)
- Intent (what did the principal ask the agent to do?)
- Authority (which actions may the agent take?)
- Constraints (budget, timing, counterparty limits)
- Payments (what value may the agent move?)
- Trust (can the counterparty verify the agent and its authority?)
- Auditability (can everyone prove what was requested, authorized, and executed?)

The key observation: intelligence sits at the top of this stack, not the bottom. A capable model with no identity, no authority, and no payment rail can't do much in the world. A less capable model with all of those layers can.

**The billion-agent problem** — the next internet has to accommodate not just billions of people but enormous numbers of agents acting for them. One person may have many agents. One business may operate hundreds or thousands. A university might run institutional, departmental, course-level, and learner agents simultaneously. The commercial internet was designed for human-scale traffic. The agentic internet needs different infrastructure.

**The new scarcity** — as intelligence becomes abundant (anyone can run a capable model), the scarce assets become: trust, permission, verified identity, authentic relationships, and proprietary context. Agents commoditize execution. Permissioned relationships become more valuable.

## What we built on top of the thesis

**Main & Meta Inc.** — the holding company for the thesis and its implementations. The positioning: don't rebuild the rails (Visa, Mastercard, Google, AI foundation models are the rails). Build the relationship layer above them.

**Student Center** — learning, credentials, achievement, and institutional identity, with ERC-721 credentials minted on Base. The thesis applied to professional education: a credential that's machine-readable, portable, and verifiable without calling the issuing institution.

**AvenueD / dPlaza** — a decentralized community platform. The agentic economy needs places where communities and their agents interact. dPlaza is building that infrastructure.

**Zeitgeist Intelligence Engine** — behavioral and cultural intelligence: understanding the emotional and identity signals underneath consumer behavior. If the agentic economy is about trust and authentic relationships, knowing what actually resonates with people — "the feeling behind the behavior" — is the intelligence layer on top.

**The Main & Meta voice agent** — an embodiment of the thesis. A 24/7 voice AI that can have a substantive conversation about the agentic economy, what Main & Meta is building, and why it matters. The agent IS the product, demonstrating the product.

## The honest version

We believe this framing is right. We also know the gap between "the thesis is correct" and "we've built the winning company in this space" is enormous. The thesis tells you what matters. It doesn't tell you which product, which distribution channel, or which partnership closes the gap.

What we do know: we've been building on this thesis long enough to understand its failure modes, and we're still standing. The companies that survive the transition to the agentic economy will be the ones that understood early that trust and permission were the scarce assets — and started accumulating them before everyone else noticed.
